# Voice profile — avneh (AmadorUAVs)

Derived from how he actually writes when directing work, not from polished prose.
Sample source: this repo's working conversation. Grammar and spelling are
deliberately normalized for published copy at his instruction ("use proper
grammar though") — the *rhythm and stance* below are what carry over, not the
lowercase and typos.

## Sentence mechanics

- **Short and declarative.** Most sentences are one clause. He front-loads the
  point and stops. "The mesh is fucked, contrast is too low." Subject, verb,
  done.
- **Comma-linked clauses**, not subordination. He stacks two or three related
  facts with commas rather than building a periodic sentence. In published copy
  this becomes short sentences side by side, or a comma splice tightened into a
  clean compound sentence.
- **No em dashes. No semicolons. Ever.** He does not use them once. If a draft
  reaches for an em dash, use a comma, a period, or parentheses.
- **Parentheses are his aside of choice.** Very frequent, always a clarifier or
  a caveat: "(copy zeph but alter words)", "(still very slow)", "(fake sun
  casting light onto the drone almost)". Keep this habit — it is the single most
  distinctive punctuation move he has.
- Length variance is real but narrow: mostly 6–18 words, occasionally one long
  run-on when he is listing requirements.

## Diction

- Plain Anglo-Saxon words. Nothing Latinate, nothing corporate.
- **Names the exact thing.** Never "the propulsion system" — always "MN7005",
  "24-inch props", "the arm tube", "Pixhawk 6X". Concrete part numbers and
  measurements everywhere. This is the strongest signal of the voice.
- Contractions throughout.
- Compresses aggressively in chat ("smth", "zeph", "u"). In published copy this
  surfaces as *brevity*, not as abbreviation — cut the sentence, don't clip the
  word.
- ALL CAPS for emphasis on a single word ("as FAST as possible"). Sparingly
  usable in copy; mostly translate to sentence structure instead.

## Stance and energy

- **Blunt. States problems flat.** No softening, no hedging, no "we believe" or
  "we strive to". If something is bad he says it is bad. If a thing works he
  says it works.
- **No enthusiasm inflation.** He never praises his own work. Achievements get
  stated as facts and then he moves on to the next problem.
- Comfortable naming limits and failures. A page in his voice should be willing
  to say what did not work and what is still unproven.
- Asks direct questions when something does not add up: "why can't you just use
  the full res GLB". Rhetorical questions in copy are fine if they are this
  plain.
- Additive openers ("also", "then") to bolt on a new requirement. In copy this
  becomes a new short sentence, not a "Furthermore".

## What this means for site copy

Write it the way an engineer on the team would explain the aircraft to another
engineer who asked. Facts, part numbers, what was tested and what it showed.
Short sentences. Parenthetical caveats where a claim needs one. No brochure
language, no significance inflation, no three-item parallel lists, no closing
paragraph that restates the section.

## Hard bans for this voice

- Em dashes and semicolons
- "not just X, but Y" and every false antithesis
- Rule-of-three parallel lists
- Puffery: cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, world-class, passionate, dedicated,
  showcase, boasts, testament, seamless, robust, vibrant, leverage, foster
- Editorial throat-clearing: "it's important to note", "notably", "it's worth
  mentioning"
- Section-summary closing paragraphs
- Title Case Headings (use sentence case)
- Vague authority: "studies show", "experts agree"
